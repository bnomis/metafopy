    Analyses¶

    Worked examples that mirror the metafor analyses gallery. The data are the metadat datasets bundled with metafopy, so every block runs as shown. Compare the output with the metafor pages to see that the numbers agree.

    BCG vaccine against tuberculosis¶

    Colditz et al. (1994), https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.1994.03510330076038, and the analysis of Berkey et al. (1995), https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.4780140406. Trials compared vaccinated and unvaccinated subjects. The effect size is the log risk ratio.

    import matplotlib

matplotlib.use('Agg')

import matplotlib.pyplot
import numpy

from metafopy import confint, datasets, escalc, forest, funnel, predict, rma

dat = escalc(measure='RR', ai='tpos', bi='tneg', ci='cpos', di='cneg', data=datasets.load('dat.bcg'))
res = rma('yi', 'vi', data=dat)
print(res)
print(predict(res, transf=numpy.exp))

    The estimated risk ratio falls steadily with absolute latitude: the vaccine does little near the equator and cuts the risk by well over half in the north and south. The moderator accounts for most of the heterogeneity. Allocation method is another candidate moderator.     Beta blockers after myocardial infarction (Yusuf, 1985)¶

    Yusuf et al. (1985), https://doi.org/10.1016/S0033-0620(85)80003-7, described Peto's one-step method for pooling 2x2 tables of rare events. The dataset holds several tables of the article; this one is total mortality in short-term trials of oral beta blockers:

    from metafopy import rma_mh, rma_peto

dat = datasets.load('dat.yusuf1985')
print(rma_peto(ai='ai', n1i='n1i', ci='ci', n2i='n2i', data=dat, subset=dat['table'] == '6'))

    Hospital stay of stroke patients¶

    Teacher expectancy and IQ¶

    Raudenbush (1984), https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.76.1.85, synthesised experiments on whether teachers' expectations affect pupils' IQ. The effect sizes are standardized mean differences. The studies differ in the number of weeks of contact between teacher and pupils before the expectancy was induced, which may moderate the effect:

    dat = datasets.load('dat.raudenbush1985')
print(rma('yi', 'vi', data=dat))
print(rma('yi', 'vi', mods='~ weeks', data=dat))

    The data are from Hackshaw et al. (1997), https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.315.7114.980, on the risk of lung cancer in women exposed to tobacco smoke by a smoking spouse. The regression test is significant but the rank correlation test is not, trim and fill imputes studies on the left of the funnel and lowers the pooled estimate, and the selection model does not reject the absence of selection. Such mixed answers are typical. See Publication bias.

    Multilevel meta-analysis of recidivism and mental health¶

    Assink and Wibbelink (2016), https://doi.org/10.20982/tqmp.12.3.p154, use studies on the association between recidivism and mental health in delinquent juveniles. Studies contribute several effect sizes each, so a three-level model has random effects for studies and for effect sizes within studies. The sampling errors of the effect sizes of one study are correlated. The correlation is not reported, so the sampling variance-covariance matrix assumes 0.6, and cluster-robust inference protects against a wrong assumption:

    from metafopy import rma_mv, robust, vcalc

dat = datasets.load('dat.assink2016')
V = vcalc(vi=dat['vi'], cluster=dat['study'], obs=dat['esid'], rho=0.6)
res = rma_mv(yi='yi', V=V, random='~ 1 | study/esid', data=dat)
print(res)
print(robust(res, cluster=dat['study']))

    Konstantopoulos (2011), https://doi.org/10.1002/jrsm.35, analysed studies of the effect of modified school calendars on student achievement. Each study is a school, and schools are nested in districts:

res = rma_mv(yi='yi', V='vi', random='~ 1 | district/school', data=dat)

    Writing-to-learn interventions: a location-scale model¶

    dat = datasets.load('dat.bangertdrowns2004')
print(rma('yi', 'vi', mods='~ length', data=dat))
