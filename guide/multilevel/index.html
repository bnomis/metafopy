    Multilevel and multivariate models¶

    rma_mv fits models in which the effect sizes are not independent: several effect sizes per study, studies nested in districts, several outcomes measured in the same subjects, effects that share a sampling error or are correlated through a phylogeny. It mirrors metafor's rma.mv and follows the interface closely:

    • yi: the effect sizes, and V: their sampling variances or the whole variance-covariance matrix.
    • random: one or more formulas of the form ~ 1 | factor (random intercepts) or ~ inner | outer (a structured variance-covariance matrix). Nested factors are written ~ 1 | district/school.
    • struct: the structure of the variance-covariance matrix for ~ inner | outer terms.

    A three-level model¶

    The schools data of Konstantopoulos (2011), https://doi.org/10.1002/jrsm.35, have studies (schools) nested in districts:

    from metafopy import datasets, rma_mv

dat = datasets.load('dat.konstantopoulos2011')
res = rma_mv(yi='yi', V='vi', random='~ 1 | district/school', data=dat)
print(res)

    Several effect sizes per study¶

    When a study contributes several effect sizes, the sampling errors are correlated. If the correlation is unknown, assume one and build the sampling variance-covariance matrix with vcalc, then check the sensitivity of the conclusions to it:

    from metafopy import vcalc

dat = datasets.load('dat.assink2016')
V = vcalc(vi=dat['vi'], cluster=dat['study'], obs=dat['esid'], rho=0.6)
res = rma_mv(yi='yi', V=V, random='~ 1 | study/esid', data=dat)
print(res)

    Moderators¶

    Moderators work as in rma_uni, with mods:

    res = rma_mv(yi='yi', V=V, mods='~ year', random='~ 1 | study/esid', data=dat)
print(res)

    Cluster-robust inference¶

    If the dependence structure is only approximately right, use cluster-robust (sandwich) inference. robust replaces the standard errors, tests and confidence intervals of a fit, with the study as the cluster (Hedges et al., 2010, https://doi.org/10.1002/jrsm.5):

    from metafopy import robust

print(robust(res, cluster=dat['study']))

    Confidence intervals for variance components¶

    from metafopy import confint

print(confint(rma_mv(yi='yi', V='vi', random='~ 1 | study/esid', data=dat), sigma2=1))

               estimate   ci.lb   ci.ub
sigma^2.2    0.1120  0.0671  0.1810
sigma.2      0.3347  0.2590  0.4255

    The profile-likelihood interval for the component with 0-based index sigma2=1 is computed. Without an index, intervals for all components are returned.

    Optimiser and Hessian settings¶

    control= of rma_mv takes the arguments of metafor: optimizer ('nlminb' by default, here BFGS with the analytic score; 'optim' with optmethod, 'nlm', 'bobyqa', 'hjk' and the others of rma.mv are run on the log-likelihood alone, optim's BFGS and Nelder-Mead as exact ports and the rest with the closest SciPy method), ncpus, parallel and cl (accepted, but the likelihood is always evaluated serially), mfmaxit (the number of likelihood evaluations after which the fit stops with an error), nearpd (force the G, H and M matrices to be positive definite, which the cvvc example of ?matreg needs to obtain res.vvc) and hesspack ('numDeriv', 'pracma' or 'calculus') with hessianCtrl for the Hessians of optbeta and cvvc.

    Known correlation matrices¶

    For phylogenetic and other known structures, give the correlation matrix as R={'species.id': matrix}: a dict of DataFrames whose row and column names match the levels of the grouping factor. The bundled dat.moura2021 comes with its phylogenetic tree (Moura et al., 2021), and PhyloTree.vcv turns the tree into a correlation matrix, as ape::vcv does in R. This model has many effect sizes and takes a while to fit:

    # docs: skip
tree = datasets.load_tree('dat.moura2021.tree')
dat = escalc(measure='ZCOR', ri='ri', ni='ni', data=datasets.load('dat.moura2021'))
dat['obs'] = range(1, len(dat) + 1)
res = rma_mv(yi='yi', V='vi', random=['~ 1 | species.id', '~ 1 | study.id', '~ 1 | obs'], R={'species.id': tree.vcv(corr=True)}, data=dat)
print(res)

    See the R argument of rma_mv.

    What works on an rma_mv fit¶

    predict, anova, confint (profile-likelihood intervals for the variance components), profile, ranef, robust, cooks_distance, dfbetas, rstandard and rstudent accept an rma_mv fit, as does its hatvalues() method. The case-deletion diagnostics refit the model without each observation, or without each cluster given as cluster=:

    from metafopy import cooks_distance, ranef, rstudent

dat = datasets.load('dat.konstantopoulos2011')
res = rma_mv(yi='yi', V='vi', random='~ 1 | district/school', data=dat)
print(cooks_distance(res, cluster='district'))
print(rstudent(res, cluster='district'))
print(ranef(res)['district'])

    As in metafor, influence, leave1out and qqnorm are not available for rma_mv fits, see Diagnostics.
