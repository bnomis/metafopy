    Binary and count data¶

    For 2x2 tables (events and non-events in a treatment and a control group) and for event counts with person-time, the data can be analysed in several ways. All of them take the raw counts, so you do not call escalc yourself.

    Function Method Measures
    rma_uni Inverse-variance, on escalc effect sizes any
    rma_mh Mantel-Haenszel OR, RR, RD, IRR, IRD
    rma_peto Peto's one-step method OR
    rma_glmm Generalized linear mixed-effects models OR, IRR, PLO, IRLN and the experimental measures

    The arguments are the same throughout: ai, bi, ci, di for the cells of the table (events and non-events in the treatment and control group), or ai, n1i, ci, n2i with the group sizes, and data=.

    Mantel-Haenszel¶

    Mantel-Haenszel pooling (Mantel and Haenszel, 1959, https://doi.org/10.1093/jnci/22.4.719) behaves well with sparse data, and needs no continuity correction for studies with a zero in a single cell. It is an equal-effects method.

    from metafopy import datasets, rma_mh

bcg = datasets.load('dat.bcg')
print(rma_mh(ai='tpos', bi='tneg', ci='cpos', di='cneg', data=bcg, measure='RR'))

    Peto's method¶

    Peto's method (Yusuf et al., 1985, https://doi.org/10.1016/S0033-0620(85)80003-7) works on the observed minus expected number of events and is accurate for rare events and balanced groups:

    from metafopy import rma_peto

print(rma_peto(ai='tpos', bi='tneg', ci='cpos', di='cneg', data=bcg))

    Generalized linear mixed-effects models¶

    rma_glmm fits the exact binomial or Poisson likelihood with random effects, which avoids the normal approximation and the continuity corrections of the inverse-variance method. This is the best choice for sparse data, and it is slower. Choose the model with model: 'UM.FS' and 'UM.RS' are unconditional models with fixed or random study effects, 'CM.AL' and 'CM.EL' are conditional models (approximate and exact likelihood). See Stijnen et al. (2010), https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.4040.

    from metafopy import rma_glmm

print(rma_glmm(ai='tpos', bi='tneg', ci='cpos', di='cneg', data=bcg, measure='OR', model='UM.FS'))

    rma_glmm accepts moderators (mods='~ ablat'). predict, blup and ranef work on its result. Mixed-model fits can differ slightly from R, because lme4 stops at a looser optimiser tolerance; see Differences from metafor.

    As in metafor, control={'package': 'GLMMadaptive'} fits the mixed models with adaptive Gauss-Hermite quadrature (Pinheiro and Bates, 1995; GLMMadaptive) and control={'package': 'glmmTMB'} with the Laplace approximation (Brooks et al., 2017); nAGQ is ignored by glmmTMB.

    For the conditional model CM.EL with method='FE', control={'optimizer': 'clogit'} or 'clogistic' fit it with the partial likelihood of survival::clogit or Epi::clogistic. link= and family= change the link function or the family of the unconditional models.

    The other optimisers of rma.glmm are available for CM.EL too (control={'optimizer': 'BFGS'}, 'Nelder-Mead', or 'optim' with optmethod=, optCtrl={'maxit': ...}, and 'CG', 'L-BFGS-B', 'nlm', 'bobyqa', ...). optim's BFGS and Nelder-Mead are exact ports of R's routines; the rest use the closest SciPy method. control={'hesspack': 'pracma'} or 'calculus' (default 'numDeriv') and control={'hessianCtrl': {...}} choose how the Hessian of CM.EL is computed. control={'dnchgcalc': 'dnoncenhypergeom'} or 'dFNCHypergeo' and control={'dnchgprec': 1e-10} choose the density of the non-central hypergeometric distribution; naming 'dFNCHypergeo' or a precision uses a port of BiasedUrn's algorithm, including the truncation of its tails. See differences from metafor for what can differ.

    Rates¶

    For incidence rates, pass the event counts x1i, x2i and the person-time t1i, t2i, with measure='IRR':

    from metafopy import escalc

print(escalc(measure='IRR', x1i=[3, 5], t1i=[100, 120], x2i=[7, 4], t2i=[110, 90])[['yi', 'vi']])

             yi       vi
0 -0.751988  0.47619
1 -0.064539  0.45000

    Zero cells¶

    Studies with a zero cell need a continuity correction for the inverse-variance method. add, to and drop00 control it and have the same meaning in escalc, rma_uni, rma_mh, rma_peto and rma_glmm. Studies with no events in both groups carry no information about the relative effect and are dropped by rma_mh and rma_peto (drop00=True). Sweeting et al. (2004), https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.1761, discuss the alternatives.
