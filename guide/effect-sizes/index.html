    Effect sizes¶

    Most analyses start from raw study data (2x2 tables, means and standard deviations, correlations, proportions) that escalc converts to an effect size yi and its sampling variance vi. If the studies already report effect sizes and standard errors, skip escalc and pass them to rma directly.

    escalc¶

    escalc takes a measure and the arguments that measure needs, and returns a pandas DataFrame. With data= the new columns yi and vi are appended to your data.

    from metafopy import datasets, escalc

dat = datasets.load('dat.curtis1998')
dat = escalc(measure='ROM', m1i='m1i', sd1i='sd1i', n1i='n1i', m2i='m2i', sd2i='sd2i', n2i='n2i', data=dat)
print(dat[['id', 'genus', 'species', 'yi', 'vi']].head())

       id    genus    species        yi        vi
0  21    ALNUS      RUBRA  0.550192  0.038472
1  22    ALNUS      RUBRA  0.147040  0.017455
2  27     ACER     RUBRUM  0.437769  0.032753
3  32  QUERCUS     PRINUS -0.110832  0.029516
4  35    MALUS  DOMESTICA  0.117135  0.046786

    Here ROM is the log ratio of means, comparing woody plant mass under elevated and ambient CO2, the example of Hedges et al. (1999), https://doi.org/10.1890/0012-9658(1999)080[1150:TMAORR]2.0.CO;2. A ratio of means suits outcomes that are positive and measured on a ratio scale.

    The measures are metafor's, with the same abbreviations, and fall into groups by the data they need:

    Data Arguments Measures, for example
    2x2 tables ai, bi, ci, di (or n1i, n2i with ai, ci) RR, OR, RD, PETO, AS, PHI, YUQ, RTET
    Person-time x1i, x2i, t1i, t2i IRR, IRD, IRSD
    Two group means m1i, m2i, sd1i, sd2i, n1i, n2i MD, SMD, SMDH, ROM, VR, CVR
    Correlations ri, ni COR, ZCOR
    Proportions xi, ni PR, PLO, PAS, PFT
    Incidence rates xi, ti IR, IRLN, IRS, IRFT
    Single means mi, sdi, ni MN, MNLN, SMN, SDLN, CVLN
    Change scores m1i, m2i, sd1i, sd2i, ni, ri MC, SMCC, SMCR, ROMC
    Partial and semi-partial correlations, R^2 ri, ti, ni, mi, r2i PCOR, SPCOR, R2, ZR2
    Precomputed yi, vi or sei GEN

    metafopy.effects.measures.MEASURES is the full list, and the escalc reference the required arguments of each. The metafor documentation describes the formulas: https://wviechtb.github.io/metafor/reference/escalc.html.

    Zero cells and bias correction¶

    Counts of zero make log ratios undefined. add, to and drop00 control the continuity correction exactly as in metafor: by default 0.5 is added to all four cells of a study that has a zero cell (to='only0'). Set add=0 to turn this off, or to='all' to correct every study.

    from metafopy import escalc

zero = datasets.load('dat.hine1989')
print(escalc(measure='OR', ai='ai', n1i='n1i', ci='ci', n2i='n2i', data=zero)[['yi', 'vi']])
print(escalc(measure='OR', ai='ai', n1i='n1i', ci='ci', n2i='n2i', data=zero, add=0)[['yi', 'vi']])

    Measures such as SMD apply the small-sample bias correction of Hedges (1981), https://doi.org/10.3102/10769986006002107, by default (correct=True). For the choice of sampling variance formula see vtype, described in the metafor documentation.

    Correlations and proportions¶

    The raw correlation has a variance that depends on the correlation itself. Meta-analyses of correlations therefore work on Fisher's z (ZCOR) and back-transform for presentation, following Borenstein et al. (2009), https://doi.org/10.1002/9780470743386:

    import numpy

from metafopy import predict, rma

mol = datasets.load('dat.molloy2014')
mol = escalc(measure='ZCOR', ri='ri', ni='ni', data=mol)
res = rma('yi', 'vi', data=mol)
print(predict(res, transf=numpy.tanh))

         pred   ci.lb   ci.ub   pi.lb   pi.ub
0  0.1488  0.0878  0.2087 -0.0371  0.3248

    For proportions, the logit (PLO) is a common choice, with transf.ilogit to go back:

    from metafopy import transf

pri = datasets.load('dat.pritz1997')
pri = escalc(measure='PLO', xi='xi', ni='ni', data=pri)
print(predict(rma('yi', 'vi', data=pri), transf=transf.ilogit))

         pred   ci.lb   ci.ub   pi.lb   pi.ub
0  0.7575  0.6605  0.8337  0.4661  0.9179

    Precomputed effect sizes¶

    If a dataset already has effect sizes and sampling variances, pass them straight to rma, or to escalc with measure='GEN' to attach labels. Standard errors work too, with sei=:

    from metafopy import rma

rau = datasets.load('dat.raudenbush1985')
print(rma('yi', 'vi', data=rau))

    Study labels, subsets and flipping¶

    slab gives study labels used in plots and printed output, subset selects studies, and flip changes the sign of the effect size of selected studies. All three accept a column name or an array.

    stay = escalc(measure='MD', n1i='n1i', n2i='n2i', m1i='m1i', m2i='m2i', sd1i='sd1i', sd2i='sd2i', data=datasets.load('dat.normand1999'), slab='source')
print(stay.attrs['slab'])

    ['Edinburgh', 'Orpington-Mild', 'Orpington-Moderate', 'Orpington-Severe', 'Montreal-Home', 'Montreal-Transfer', 'Newcastle', 'Umea', 'Uppsala']

    Transformations¶

    metafopy.transf holds metafor's transformation functions (transf.ilogit, transf.ztor, transf.dtocles and so on, also imported at the top level as transf_ilogit, transf_ztor, ...). Pass them as transf= to predict and confint, or as atransf= to the plot functions. NumPy functions such as numpy.exp and numpy.tanh work as well.
